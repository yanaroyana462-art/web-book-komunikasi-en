In the professional world, organizations, or social circles, we frequently encounter differing opinions, clashes of ego, and conflicts. Rigid and arrogant communication in the workplace can ruin professionalism and harm team morale. Meanwhile, using the wrong tone of voice with friends can fracture close bonds built over many years.

Islam never separates religious ethics from professional and social etiquette. The way we engage in discussions, correct colleagues, critique ideas, and appreciate team contributions is a direct reflection of our moral maturity.

> <p class="arabic">وَشَاوِرْهُمْ فِي الْأَمْرِ ۖ فَإِذَا عَزَمْتَ فَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى اللَّهِ</p>
>
> *"And consult them in the matter. Then when you have taken a decision, put your trust in Allah."*  
> — **QS. Ali 'Imran [3]: 159**

## 4 Ethics of Professional Communication Inspired by the Prophet ﷺ

As a leader and a devoted friend, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ managed his team (his companions) with a balanced combination of firmness, respect, openness, and warmth.

### 1. Prioritizing Consultation & Inclusive Communication
Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was never authoritarian, never assumed he knew best, nor did he impose his will in technical worldly affairs. He consistently opened spaces for discussion, welcomed ideas, and adopted input from team members regardless of seniority or social status.

<div class="kisah-box">

> Ahead of the Battle of the Trench, the city of Medina faced an impending attack from a massive coalition army. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ did not unilaterally decide on a defense strategy. Instead, he gathered his companions to consult. 
> 
> Salman Al-Farisi, an ordinary companion from Persia, proposed an idea unprecedented in Arabian military tactics: digging a vast trench along the city border. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ listened intently, appreciated the insight, and directly led the team to execute the plan, which ultimately saved Medina (HR. Ahmad).

</div>

**Daily Application:**
* **In the Workplace:** Conduct inclusive *brainstorming* sessions, give junior team members opportunities to speak, and value fresh ideas.
* **In Organizations:** Avoid dominant attitudes that suppress constructive discussion when making collective decisions.

---

### 2. Correcting Without Humiliating (Private & Educational Feedback)
Rebuking a colleague's mistake in public only fuels egos, breeds resentment, and harms team morale. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ always safeguarded the dignity of others when offering feedback, carefully choosing the right time and place to deliver educational corrections.

<div class="kisah-box">

> When Abu Dharr Al-Ghifari radhiyallahu 'anhu inadvertently insulted another companion by disparaging his mother's background as a former slave, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ corrected him privately one-on-one: 
> 
> *"O Abu Dharr, did you insult him by mentioning his mother? Indeed, you still retain traits of ignorance (Jahiliyyah)."* 
> 
> Upon hearing this private correction, Abu Dharr recognized his fault, wept, and placed his cheek on the ground to seek forgiveness (HR. Bukhari no. 30). The Prophet addressed the action and behavior without dismantling Abu Dharr's personal dignity.

</div>

**Daily Application:**
* **Performance Evaluation (*Feedback*):** Share project corrections or critiques privately (*one-on-one*), rather than in group chats or large meetings.
* **Focusing on Solutions:** Address errors in the work (*task*), rather than launching personal attacks (*ad hominem*).

---

### 3. Acknowledging & Appreciating Others' Contributions
A good leader and friend never claims team success as a personal achievement. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ consistently acknowledged the potential, contributions, and hard work of his companions in a fair and transparent manner.

> <p class="arabic">لَا يَشْكُرُ اللَّهَ مَنْ لَا يَشْكُرُ النَّاسَ</p>
>
> *"He who does not thank people does not thank Allah."*  
> — **HR. Abu Dawud no. 4811**

<div class="kisah-box">

> Prophet Muhammad ﷺ frequently bestowed uplifting and appreciative honorifics upon his companions to boost their morale according to their respective strengths and capabilities. 
> 
> Khalid bin Walid was named *"Saifullah"* (Drawn Sword of Allah), Abu Ubaidah bin Al-Jarrah was titled *"Aminul Ummah"* (Trustee of this Nation), and Ali bin Abi Thalib was honored as *"Babul Ilm"* (Gate of Knowledge). This verbal appreciation ensured every team member felt valued and vital.

</div>

**Daily Application:**
* **Culture of Appreciation:** Build a habit of giving open praise (*public praise*) for the hard work of colleagues or team members.
* **Sharing Credit:** Give proper credit to all team members whenever a project achieves success.

---

### 4. Holding No Grudges & Protecting Team Confidentiality
Differing opinions or minor friction within a team are natural. However, gossiping about colleagues behind their backs (*ghibah*) or breaching internal team confidences damages essential trust (*trust*).

> <p class="arabic">إِذَا حَدَّثَ الرَّجُلُ بِالْحَدِيثِ ثُمَّ الْتَفَتَ فَهِيَ أَمَانَةٌ</p>
>
> *"When a person tells you something and then looks around, it is a trust (confidential)."*  
> — **HR. Abu Dawud no. 4868**

<div class="kisah-box">

> Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught his companions to strictly honor privacy and confidentiality in interactions. He firmly prohibited *namimah* (tale-bearing) or speaking ill of colleagues in their absence, as such behavior breaks unity.

</div>

**Daily Application:**
* **Respecting Privacy:** Keep internal meeting information or personal confidences safe from becoming workplace gossip.
* **Direct Resolution:** If dissatisfied with a colleague, resolve the issue directly and politely without spreading negative opinions to others.

## Simple Real-Life Practice

Let us compare communication approaches in everyday work and organizational environments:

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Providing Feedback / Work Evaluation">
    **Less Ideal Way (Personal Attack):**  
    "You are so unprofessional! You can't even handle a simple task like this; you're ruining the team's reputation!"

    ---

    **Prophetic Way (Constructive & Dignified):**  
    *(Discussing privately)* "There are a few points in this report that we need to refine together. I know you put effort into this; let's restructure this section so the result aligns with our goal."
  </TabItem>

  <TabItem label="Differing Opinions in Meetings">
    **Less Ideal Way (Self-Centered & Aggressive):**  
    "Your idea makes no sense at all! We should stick to my approach, which is already proven to work!"

    ---

    **Prophetic Way (Consultative & Open):**  
    "That's an interesting perspective. However, let's also evaluate the potential risks in point B. What do the rest of you think is the safest and most efficient option for us?"
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Team & Friendship Communication Checklist

:::tip[Reflecting on Professional Speech]
Before wrapping up your work today, take a moment to review these 3 points:

1. **Appreciation:** Have I thanked the colleagues who assisted me with my tasks today?
2. **Correction:** If a team member made a mistake, did I address it privately or embarrass them publicly?
3. **Integrity:** Did any of my words, sarcastic comments, or assumptions hurt a colleague or team member today?
:::

> **Closing Message:**  
> Great teams are not formed by groups of people who never make mistakes, but by individuals who respect one another, practice open consultation, and uphold mutual honor through courteous and mature speech.
