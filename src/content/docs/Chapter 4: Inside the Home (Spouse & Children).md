Sometimes we can be exceptionally friendly, patient, and full of smiles toward friends or strangers outside. Yet, the moment we step inside our home, we easily succumb to emotions, speak in raised voices, and rarely offer praise or appreciation to our own spouse and children.

However, the home is the true testing ground for communication. A person's character and faith can be measured by how they treat their family when no one else is watching.

> <p class="arabic">خَيْرُكُمْ خَيْرُكُمْ لِأَهْلِهِ وَأَنَا خَيْرُكُمْ لِأَهْلِي</p>
>
> *"The best of you are those who are best to their families, and I am the best among you to my family."*  
> — **HR. Tirmidzi no. 3895**

---

## 4 Principles of Family Communication Inspired by the Prophet ﷺ

Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was a husband, father, and grandfather who was immensely warm and full of affection. He never positioned himself as a rigid, intimidating head of the household, nor did he make the home atmosphere tense and high-pressured.

### 1. Using Endearing Terms & Addressing with Respect
Calling a spouse or child with soft, pleasing terms can thaw tension, foster a sense of mutual belonging, and cultivate care and respect within the household.

<div class="kisah-box">

> Prophet Muhammad ﷺ frequently called Mother Aisyah radhiyallahu 'anha with affectionate names like *"O Aish"* or *"O Humaira"* (the one with rosy cheeks) to tease her lovingly and bring joy to her heart (HR. Muslim no. 2440). 
> 
> He taught that sweet words and special endearments inside the home serve as essential "fuel" to maintain harmony and warmth in a relationship.

</div>

**Daily Application:**
* **For a Spouse:** Use preferred terms of endearment instead of formal names or cold titles.
* **For Children:** Call children with encouraging terms that build their self-confidence, such as *"My Champion", "Bright Child",* or *"Father's Virtuous Daughter"*.

---

### 2. Sharing Humor & Creating a Cheerful Atmosphere
Prophet Muhammad ﷺ never allowed the home atmosphere to become somber or stiffly formal. He constantly made time to joke, banter, and play with his spouse and grandchildren, creating deep emotional bonds.

<div class="kisah-box">

> Prophet Muhammad ﷺ once challenged Mother Aisyah to a footrace while they were traveling with a caravan. On their first race, Aisyah won. 
> 
> Years later, when Aisyah had gained some weight, the Prophet invited her to race again, and this time he won. He laughed joyfully and said warmly: *"This victory pays back my former defeat!"* (HR. Abu Dawud no. 2578).

</div>

**Daily Application:**
* **Amid Busy Schedules:** Insert simple humor or playful moments with a spouse and children after returning from work to unwind from daily fatigue.
* **Removing Rigidity:** Avoid acting overly dominant so family members feel free to laugh and share stories openly at home.

---

### 3. Refraining from Criticizing Food or Household Endeavors
Conflicts and arguments within a household are often triggered by trivial matters, such as a dish not tasting quite right or the house being messy. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ provided an extraordinary example in safeguarding feelings and appreciating the effort of family members.

> <p class="arabic">مَا عَابَ النَّبِيُّ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ طَعَامًا قَطُّ إِنِ اشْتَهَاهُ أَكَلَهُ وَإِلَّا تَرَكَهُ</p>
>
> *"The Prophet ﷺ never criticized any food at all. If he liked it, he ate it; and if he disliked it, he left it (without harsh remarks)."*  
> — **HR. Bukhari no. 5409**

<div class="kisah-box">

> If a meal did not suit his taste, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ chose silence rather than launching harsh criticism that could hurt the feelings of the person who worked hard to prepare it. He deeply appreciated the effort and care, rather than focusing solely on the end result.

</div>

**Daily Application:**
* **Appreciating Effort:** Express gratitude for meals prepared or household chores completed by a spouse or child, even if the outcome is not perfect.
* **Avoiding Harsh Criticism:** If there is disappointment, convey feedback privately with polite words without a judgmental tone.

---

### 4. Openly Expressing Affection (*Expressive Affection*)
Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was never hesitant or embarrassed to show affection—whether through sweet words or physical touch—to his spouse and children, even in public settings.

<div class="kisah-box">

> One day, a tribal chieftain named Al-Aqra' bin Habis saw Prophet Muhammad ﷺ kiss his grandson, Al-Hasan. Surprised, Al-Aqra' said: *"I have 10 children, and I have never kissed a single one of them."* 
> 
> Prophet Muhammad ﷺ looked at him and replied firmly: *"Whoever does not show mercy will not be shown mercy."* (HR. Bukhari no. 5997). He emphasized that gentleness and outward affection toward family are signs of divine mercy in a person's heart.

</div>

**Daily Application:**
* **Words of Affirmation:** Get into the habit of saying *"I love you"* or *"Thank you for working hard today"* to a spouse and children.
* **Physical Touch:** Offer warm hugs, kisses on the forehead, or hold hands with a spouse and children before leaving and upon returning home.

## Simple Real-Life Practice

Let us compare everyday communication patterns at home when facing emotionally charged situations:

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="When Food or Household Service Falls Short">
    **Less Ideal Way (Hurtful):**  
    "Why is this food so salty? Do you even care about cooking for your spouse?"

    ---

    **Prophetic Way (Safeguarding Feelings):**  
    *(Eats modestly without complaining)* Or says gently: "Thank you for putting effort into cooking today. It's a bit rich in flavor, maybe tomorrow we can ease up slightly on the salt."
  </TabItem>

  <TabItem label="When a Child Spills Water / Makes a Mistake">
    **Less Ideal Way (Yelling):**  
    "What is wrong with you! Always messing around and creating more work for your parents!"

    ---

    **Prophetic Way (Educational & Patient):**  
    *(Pauses to calm down for 3 seconds)* "It's alright, let's wipe the water up together. Next time, make sure to hold the glass with both hands so it doesn't spill."
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Daily Family Communication Checklist

:::tip[Reflecting on Family Interactions]
Before going to sleep tonight, reflect on these 3 points:
1. **Have I addressed my spouse/children with affectionate words today?**
2. **Did any harsh words or shouting slip from my mouth inside the home?**
3. **Have I appreciated small acts of kindness done by my spouse or children today?**
:::

> **Closing Message:**  
> The beauty of our speech outside means very little if those closest to us inside our home fall victim to the harshness of our tongue. Let us make our home a sweet sanctuary (*Baiti Jannati*) through words filled with love.
