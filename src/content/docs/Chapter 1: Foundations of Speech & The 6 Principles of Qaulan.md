Have you ever re-checked your chat history on messaging apps or recalled a conversation from earlier today, only to ask yourself: *"Did I sound too pretentious?"*, *"Did my words hurt their feelings?"*, or *"Why did I even bring that up?"*

We sometimes speak thoughtlessly (*asbun*) without deep reflection, without considering the impact on the listener, or get trapped in arguments merely to win, boast, or show off our intellect.

Yet in Islam, speech is not merely a medium for daily communication. Whatever comes out of our mouths—and even whatever we type on a keyboard—is a mirror of our thoughts and feelings. An Arabic proverb states *al-lisaanu mizaanul insan*, which means: *"A person's speech is the measure of their character."*

> <p class="arabic">مَا يَلْفِظُ مِنْ قَوْلٍ إِلَّا لَدَيْهِ رَقِيبٌ عَتِيدٌ</p>
>
> *"Not a word does he utter but there is a watcher by him, ready (to record it)."*  
> — **QS. Qaf [50]: 18**

## Speech: A Mirror of the Soul's Depth

Guarding one's speech does not mean remaining passive or silently helpless. Guarding speech is a state of full mindfulness in controlling our words and taking responsibility for the impact of every statement we make. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ provided the foundational standard for speech through his tradition:

> <p class="arabic">مَنْ كَانَ يُؤْمِنُ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الآخِرِ فَلْيَقُلْ خَيْرًا أَوْ لِيَصْمُتْ</p>
>
> *"Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day should speak good or remain silent."*  
> — **HR. Bukhari no. 6018 & Muslim no. 47**

When this principle is internalized, the way we interact transforms through three core awarenesses:

1. **Words Are Investments, Not Mere Emotions.** Every word carries weight. A single sentence constructed with good intentions and the right approach can offer direction and encouragement. Conversely, careless words born from fleeting emotions often leave long-lasting wounds.
2. **Creating Safety for Others.** Others will feel secure and respected in our presence. When our speech is free from backbiting, insults, and provocation, our presence brings tranquility rather than anxiety.
3. **Focusing on Value and Impact.** The perfection of a person's faith is reflected in their ability to filter conversations. We learn to allocate our energy toward beneficial discussions rather than drowning in endless arguments.

## Emulating the 6 Speech Styles of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ

In the Quran, there are 6 distinct terms for styles of speech (*Qaulan*) practiced directly by Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. He knew precisely when to be firm, when to be gentle, and when to lighten the atmosphere.

### 1. Qaulan Sadida (Honest, Accurate & Upright Speech)
Linguistically, *Sadida* means accurate, upright, and truthful. This principle teaches us to speak truthfully, transparently, directly, and free from manipulation. In today's digital era, *Qaulan Sadida* also encompasses diligence in verifying information so we do not spread false news (*hoaxes*).

> <p class="arabic">يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَقُولُوا قَوْلًا سَدِيدًا</p>
>
> *"O you who have believed, fear Allah and speak words of appropriate justice."*  
> — **QS. Al-Ahzab [33]: 70**

<div class="kisah-box">

One day, an elderly woman came to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and asked: *"O Messenger of Allah, will an old woman like me enter Paradise?"* 

The Prophet replied with a smile: *"There are no old people in Paradise."* The woman felt sad and began to cry. 

Then, truthfully yet reassuringly, the Prophet explained: *"What I mean is that everyone who enters Paradise will be restored to their youth."* He spoke the truth even while joking (HR. Tirmidzi).

</div>

**Daily Application:**
* **Data Transparency:** Presenting facts or project status updates honestly without misrepresentation.
* **Verify Before Sharing:** Ensuring the truth of a piece of information before sharing it in group chats.

---

### 2. Qaulan Layyina (Gentle & De-escalating Speech)
*Qaulan Layyina* is the art of communicating with a soft, friendly tone that does not trigger tension. The primary goal of this style is to defuse anger, break deadlocks, and touch the listener's heart—especially when dealing with stubborn or emotional individuals.

> <p class="arabic">فَقُولَا لَهُ قَوْلًا لَّيِّنًا لَّعَلَّهُ يَتَذَكَّرُ أَوْ يَخْشَىٰ</p>
>
> *"And speak to him (Pharaoh) with gentle speech that perhaps he may be reminded or fear [Allah]."*  
> — **QS. Thaha [20]: 44**

<div class="kisah-box">

Once, a Bedouin approached and yanked the cloak of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ so harshly that it left a red mark on his neck. The man shouted rudely: *"O Muhammad, give me some of Allah's wealth that you have!"* 

The companions who witnessed this grew angry and wanted to strike the man. However, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ simply turned, smiled calmly, and instructed his companions to grant him food and wealth. His gentleness instantly defused the tense situation (HR. Bukhari no. 3149).

</div>

**Daily Application:**
* **De-escalating Conflict:** Using a calm, low tone of voice when responding to angry colleagues or partners.
* **Expressing Objections:** Expressing disagreement with polite words without coming across as aggressive.

---

### 3. Qaulan Ma'rufa (Appropriate, Polite & Culturally Sound Speech)
*Ma'rufa* refers to that which is recognized as good and appropriate by religious norms and local culture. *Qaulan Ma'rufa* consists of respectful words that uphold the dignity of the listener and refrain from harsh public criticism.

> <p class="arabic">وَقُولُوا لِلنَّاسِ حُسْنًا</p>
>
> *"And speak to people good [words]."*  
> — **QS. Al-Baqarah [2]: 83**

<div class="kisah-box">

Whenever Prophet Muhammad ﷺ noticed a mistake made by a companion, he never pointed out the individual directly in public. He would usually ascend the pulpit and speak generally:

<p class="arabic">مَا بَالُ أَقْوَامٍ يَقُولُونَ كَذَا وَكَذَا</p>

*"Why are some people saying such and such..."* (HR. Abu Dawud no. 4788).

Through this anonymous method, the individual realized their mistake without feeling humiliated in front of others.

</div>

**Daily Application:**
* **Public Etiquette:** Using polite language when interacting in public spaces or office chat groups.
* **Correction Without Humiliation:** Avoiding open criticism that damages someone's reputation.

---

### 4. Qaulan Baligha (Effective, Clear & To-the-Point Speech)
*Baligha* means reaching the mark. *Qaulan Baligha* is an effective, straightforward style of speech that gets directly to the core of the matter, is easy to comprehend, and is tailored to the background of the listener without using confusing jargon.

> <p class="arabic">وَقُل لَّهُمْ فِي أَنفُسِهِمْ قَوْلًا بَلِيغًا</p>
>
> *"And speak to them a word that reaches their souls."*  
> — **QS. An-Nisa [4]: 63**

<div class="kisah-box">

A companion named Sufyan bin Abdillah asked for a concise advice covering the essence of Islam that would be easy to practice. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ did not give a lengthy sermon; instead, he stated:

<p class="arabic">قُلْ آمَنْتُ بِاللَّهِ ثُمَّ اسْتَقِمْ</p>

*"Say: 'I believe in Allah', and then remain steadfast."* (HR. Muslim no. 38).

This brief and powerful message penetrated straight to the heart of the listener.

</div>

**Daily Application:**
* **Concise Communication:** Delivering work instructions systematically, clearly, and actionable.
* **Adapting to the Audience:** Choosing language that matches the listener's level of understanding.

---

### 5. Qaulan Karima (Noble & Respectful Speech)
*Karima* means noble. *Qaulan Karima* is speech filled with deep respect, giving proportionate honor, and elevating the status of the listener—especially when communicating with parents, elders, teachers, or leaders.

> <p class="arabic">وَقُل لَّهُمَا قَوْلًا كَرِيمًا</p>
>
> *"And speak to them a noble word."*  
> — **QS. Al-Isra [17]: 23**

<div class="kisah-box">

In his daily life, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ always made everyone he spoke with feel valued. When conversing with someone, he did not merely turn his head, but turned his entire body to face them with full attention. 

Once, an elderly woman spoke to him at length on the roadside, and Prophet Muhammad ﷺ stood patiently listening to her until she was finished.

</div>

**Daily Application:**
* **Supportive Body Language:** Giving full attention (*eye contact*) when listening to others speak.
* **Honoring Elders & Seniors:** Using polite manners and respectful language when communicating with elders.

---

### 6. Qaulan Maysura (Kind & Heart-Warming Speech)
*Maysura* stems from the word *yusr*, meaning ease or relief. *Qaulan Maysura* refers to gentle, hopeful, and comforting words—especially when we are not in a position to fulfill someone's request or request for help.

> <p class="arabic">فَقُل لَّهُمْ قَوْلًا مَّيْسُورًا</p>
>
> *"Then speak to them a gentle word."*  
> — **QS. Al-Isra [17]: 28**

<div class="kisah-box">

Whenever someone came asking for financial aid or food from Prophet Muhammad ﷺ while he had nothing to give, he never turned them away with a cold tone or dismissed them. 

He would respond with extremely soft words, pray for blessings upon them, and promise to prioritize them whenever resources became available. Consequently, the person left feeling comforted and respected.

</div>

**Daily Application:**
* **Refusing with Empathy:** Declining requests politely with clear reasons without making the requester feel disheartened.
* **Offering Alternative Solutions:** Praying for them or directing them toward other solutions if we cannot assist directly.

## Simple Real-Life Practice

Let us compare conventional speech with the Prophetic style in everyday situations:

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="When a Colleague's Report is Late">
    **Less Ideal Way (Causing Stress & Blaming):**  
    "Are you even serious about working? Your report is always late, ruining the entire team's workflow!"

    ---

    **Prophetic Way (Solution-Oriented & Empathetic):**  
    "I noticed the report isn't finished yet. Are there any technical hurdles I can help with? Let's get it done together so our team targets stay on track."
  </TabItem>

  <TabItem label="When Unable to Help a Friend">
    **Less Ideal Way (Leading On / Avoiding):**  
    "We'll see... I think I can... *(despite knowing it's impossible, then eventually ghosting)*."

    ---

    **Prophetic Way (Honest & Kind):**  
    "Thank you for trusting me. However, I'm truly sorry as my current schedule is extremely packed. I'm concerned I won't be able to deliver good results. Hopefully, I can assist you another time."
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Checklist Before Speaking

:::tip[3 Filters Before Speaking]
Before sending a message or speaking directly, pause for 3 seconds and ask yourself:

1. **Is It True?** (*Qaulan Sadida* — Free from gossip, assumptions, or hoaxes).
2. **Is It Beneficial?** (*Qaulan Baligha* — Brings goodness rather than triggering useless arguments).
3. **Is It Delivered Properly?** (*Qaulan Layyina / Ma'rufa* — Uses a polite tone that does not hurt feelings).
:::

> **Closing Message:**  
> We speak not to win arguments or to prove who is superior, but to spread goodness. May our speech be a path of virtue and salvation for us in this world and the Hereafter.
